The hardest part of posting is not writing. It is deciding what to write on a Tuesday morning with a customer waiting. A content calendar fixes that, because you decide once and then follow the plan. This Facebook content calendar template gives you a simple monthly layout, a mix of post types, a sample week, and a checklist, so you can copy it and fill it in today.

You do not need special software. A table in a document or a spreadsheet works fine. If you prefer, our free [content calendar generator](/tools/content-calendar-generator) can sketch a month for you to start from.

## What a content calendar does

A calendar is a plan that answers three questions in advance: what will I post, when, and why. It helps you in four ways.

- **It removes daily guessing.** You open the calendar and know what is next.
- **It keeps a balance.** You can see whether every post is an offer.
- **It helps you prepare.** You can take photos and gather facts ahead of time.
- **It makes teamwork easy.** Everyone sees the same plan.

Our guide to [planning a month of Facebook content](/blog/how-to-plan-a-month-of-facebook-content) explains the thinking behind a calendar in more depth. This article gives you the template.

## Choose your post types

A simple mix keeps your Page interesting. Four jobs cover most small businesses.

| Type | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Teach | Help people with a tip or answer | "Three ways to store fresh bread" |
| Show | Let people see your work or your team | A photo of today's baking |
| Offer | Invite people to buy or book | "Saturday special: two loaves for one price" |
| Ask | Start a conversation | "What is your favorite filling?" |

You can also add customer stories, news about your business, and holidays that matter to your customers.

## Build your month in five steps

1. **List your dates.** Write down holidays, events, sales, and deadlines that matter.
2. **Place your offers first.** Put them on the calendar where they belong.
3. **Fill the rest** with teach, show, and ask posts.
4. **Add the facts.** Write the prices, times, and names now, so you do not guess later.
5. **Check the balance.** If most posts are offers, swap a few for tips and questions.

For ideas to fill the gaps, read [Facebook post ideas for small businesses](/blog/facebook-post-ideas-for-small-businesses).

## Prepare ahead

The best calendar saves you work only if you prepare. Once a week, set aside an hour.

- Write the next week's posts.
- Take or choose the photos.
- Check every price and date.
- Schedule the posts for the times you chose.

## Common mistakes

- **Planning too much.** Fewer posts you can keep beat a big plan you drop.
- **Filling the calendar with offers.** Mix in value.
- **Never changing the plan.** Real life changes, so adjust the calendar.
- **Forgetting the facts.** Check prices and dates before they go out.
- **Not reviewing.** Look at the results each month and learn.

## A worked example: one month for a garden shop

It helps to see a filled-in calendar. Imagine a small garden shop planning April. It starts with fixed dates: a spring sale on the first weekend, an open day on the third Saturday, and a reminder about a delivery deadline before the holiday. These go into the table first.

Next the shop adds one teaching post each week, such as how to start seeds, how to protect plants from a late frost, and how to choose a pot. Then it adds one showing post each week, such as new stock arriving, a customer's garden, or a photo from the greenhouse. The last step is one question each week, such as "Which plant would you like to see in stock?"

By the end, the shop has about sixteen posts, and none of them required a flash of inspiration on the day. It also notices a gap in the third week, where only offers appear, and swaps one for a tip. The whole exercise takes about an hour, and it gives the owner a month of calm.

## How to adapt the template to your business

Every business has its own rhythm. A restaurant may plan around weekends and menu changes. A fitness studio may plan around class schedules and seasonal goals. An online shop may plan around product launches and shipping deadlines.

Start by changing the post types to match your world. A restaurant might use menu, behind the scenes, offer, and ask. A studio might use class highlight, member story, tip, and ask. Rename the columns if that helps, and delete any you will not use. A template should serve you, not the other way around.

Then choose how much detail each row needs. If you work alone, a short note is enough. If you work with a team, add a column for who is responsible. If you use scheduling, add one for the time. Keep the calendar light enough that you will actually open it every week.

## Keep your calendar alive

A calendar is only useful while it is current. The easiest way to keep it alive is a short weekly review. Pick a fixed day and a fixed time, such as Friday afternoon, and give yourself fifteen minutes. Look at what happened this week, look at what is coming next week, and change anything that no longer fits.

During the review, mark each post with its real status. A post that went out becomes published. A post that you skipped can move to a later day or be deleted without guilt. If something important happened in your business, such as a new product or an unexpected closure, add a post for it right away.

At the end of the month, spend a little longer. Look at your best three posts and your weakest three. Write one sentence about what each group had in common, and carry that lesson into next month's plan. Over a few months this habit turns a simple table into something much more valuable: a record of what your own customers respond to. ## Frequently asked questions

**How far ahead should I plan?** A month is a good start. Plan the details of the next week, and the outline of the rest.

**Do I need to use the same days every week?** A steady rhythm helps, but your own schedule matters more than a fixed pattern.

**Can I use this for Instagram too?** The layout works anywhere, but our tools and guides focus on Facebook Pages.

**What if I miss a day?** Move the post to the next free day. A plan should help, not scold.

**Can AI fill the calendar?** AI can suggest ideas and write drafts, but you should check the facts and read every post. Our guide to [using AI to write Facebook posts](/blog/how-to-use-ai-to-write-facebook-posts) explains how.

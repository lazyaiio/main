A real estate agent sells two things at once: a property and themselves. Buyers and sellers choose an agent they trust, and most of that trust is built before the first phone call. A Facebook Page can do a lot of that quiet work, but only if it shows more than a stream of listings. This guide gives you thirty Facebook post ideas for real estate agents, plus a sample week and a few rules for staying honest, legal, and respectful of your clients.

These ideas are our own suggestions, built from common agent routines. We cannot promise leads or sales, and your market is different from every other. Post, read your numbers, and keep what earns messages and calls. For the method behind a full month of posts, see our guide on [how to plan a month of Facebook content](/blog/how-to-plan-a-month-of-facebook-content).

## What an agent's Page is for

An agent's Page has four jobs. It shows the properties you handle. It shows that you know the area, with streets, schools, prices, and local life. It answers the questions every buyer and seller has. And it keeps you in mind for the many people who are not ready to move yet.

Because buying and selling take time, most of your readers will not act this month. Your posts earn their place by being useful when they are read, and by making you the obvious name when the time comes.

## Thirty post ideas, grouped by goal

### Show the properties (ideas 1 to 8)

1. **A new listing.** A clear lead photo, the area, the key facts, and how to arrange a viewing.
2. **A room spotlight.** One room, one good photo, and one line about what makes it work.
3. **A neighborhood view.** The street, the park, or the view from the window.
4. **An open house reminder.** Date, time, address, and what to bring.
5. **A price change.** State it plainly and accurately, with the new price.
6. **Just sold.** Share a sale only with the seller's agreement, and without private details.
7. **A feature close-up.** A kitchen detail, a garden, or a recent repair.
8. **A short walk-through video.** Keep it steady, well lit, and honest about the space.

### Show your knowledge (ideas 9 to 15)

9. **A local area guide.** Shops, transport, schools, and parks in one neighborhood.
10. **A market note in plain words.** Describe what you are seeing in your area, and say it is your own view.
11. **A glossary post.** Explain one term, such as "deposit" or "survey," in two sentences.
12. **A step-by-step guide.** The stages of buying or selling, one post per stage.
13. **A seasonal tip.** Preparing a home for a hot summer or a cold winter.
14. **A local event.** A fair, a market, or a school open day that readers might enjoy.
15. **A cost reminder.** The fees and costs that surprise first-time buyers, described in general terms.

### Show the person (ideas 16 to 22)

16. **Meet the agent.** Your background, your area, and why you do this work.
17. **A day in the job.** A few photos from a viewing, a valuation, and the office.
18. **A client thank-you.** Only with permission, and without naming anyone who prefers privacy.
19. **A lesson you learned.** One honest story about a deal, with names removed.
20. **A local business you like.** Support a cafe or shop near a property.
21. **A community involvement post.** A charity or school event you took part in.
22. **A team introduction.** The people who help you, and what each does.

### Answer common questions (ideas 23 to 30)

23. **How does a viewing work?** What happens and how to book one.
24. **How to prepare a home to sell.** Five simple tips.
25. **How to get ready to buy.** Documents, budget, and questions to ask.
26. **What does a valuation involve?** Explain the steps and the time it takes.
27. **Rent or buy?** Offer questions to think about, without telling people what to do.
28. **Common mistakes.** A short list for first-time buyers or sellers.
29. **Your service area.** Where you work, and how to contact you.
30. **A reminder to ask.** "Thinking of moving next year? Send us a message and we will talk it through."

## A sample week you can copy

Here is one example week for an agent who posts four times. Change the days to match when people browse in your area.

| Day | Post type | Idea from the list |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | A new listing or room spotlight | 1 or 2 |
| Wednesday | A local area guide or a glossary post | 9 or 11 |
| Friday | A people or behind-the-scenes post | 16 or 17 |
| Sunday | A question or a reminder | 30 or 23 |

If four posts is too many, start with two. Our guide on [how often to post on Facebook](/blog/how-often-should-you-post-on-facebook) can help you choose a rhythm you can keep.

## Rules that protect you and your clients

Property advertising is regulated in most places. The details differ by country and region, so these are general habits, not legal advice. Check your local rules and your agency's policy.

- **Get permission for photos.** Ask the owner before you post photos of their home, especially the inside.
- **Describe the property accurately.** Do not exaggerate the size, the condition, or the view.
- **Treat everyone fairly.** Keep wording free of anything that could exclude or discriminate against a group of people. Many places have strict rules on this.
- **Protect private details.** Do not post an owner's name, address history, or the reason they are selling without clear consent.
- **Label opinions as opinions.** A market note is your view, not a guarantee of future prices.
- **Do not promise returns.** Avoid claims about what a property will be worth later.
- **Show your license or agency name** where your local rules require it.

## Writing tips for listing and local posts

- **Lead with the strongest fact.** The area, the number of bedrooms, or the price.
- **Use real photos taken in good light.** Wide shots help, and so does a tidy room.
- **Keep it short.** Two to four sentences, with the details on your site or in a message.
- **Add one clear next step.** Book a viewing, message, or call.
- **Reply to comments quickly.** Our guide on [replying to Facebook comments and reviews](/blog/how-to-reply-to-facebook-comments-and-reviews) has examples.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Only posting listings.** Readers need a reason to follow you between sales.
- **Using photos you do not have rights to.** Always use your own, or those you have permission to use.
- **Posting out-of-date listings.** A sold home still shown as available costs trust.
- **Hiding the price when it is public.** Readers dislike having to ask.
- **Copying another agent's post.** Write your own.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should an agent post on Facebook?

Choose a rhythm you can keep. For many agents that is two to four posts a week. A steady schedule beats a flurry followed by silence.

### Should I post the price?

Follow your local rules and your seller's wishes. When you can show it, a clear price saves everyone's time.

### Can I use drone photos?

Rules about drones and privacy vary. Check the local laws and get the owner's agreement before you post.

### Can I plan a month of posts at once?

Yes. Choose ideas that fit your listings and your area, assign them to days, and write them in one sitting. Our free [content calendar generator](/tools/content-calendar-generator) can lay out the month for you.

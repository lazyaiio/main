A post is only half of a conversation. The other half is what happens in the comments and in the reviews. Many small businesses write careful posts and then leave the replies to chance. That is a missed chance, because a reply is often the first time a person deals with you directly. This guide shows you how to reply to Facebook comments and reviews, with example replies for praise, questions, and complaints, and a short routine to keep up with it.

The advice here comes from common customer-service practice, not from Facebook. We cannot promise any outcome, and you know your customers best. Use it as a starting point. For the bigger picture on writing for your Page, see our guide on [how to write Facebook posts that people read](/blog/how-to-write-facebook-posts-people-read).

## Why replies matter

- **They show that someone is there.** A fast, friendly reply tells people you are open for business and that you listen.
- **They answer questions for everyone.** The person who asked is not the only one reading. Others scroll past and learn from your answer.
- **They keep conversations going.** A reply gives people a reason to write back.
- **They build trust.** How you handle a problem in public says a lot about you.

You do not need to answer in seconds. You do need a habit, and a tone that sounds like a real person.

## The five rules of a good reply

1. **Be quick when you can.** Aim to answer within a day. Say so on your Page if you can only check at certain hours.
2. **Be warm and plain.** Write as you would speak to a customer at the counter.
3. **Use the person's name** when it is shown and fits.
4. **Answer the actual question.** Do not reply with a slogan.
5. **Take longer matters to a private message.** Keep the public reply short and kind.

## Replying to praise

Positive comments are easy to ignore, and that is a mistake. A short, genuine thank-you rewards the person and encourages others to speak up.

- "Thank you, Maya! We loved having you in on Saturday."
- "That means a lot to the whole team. See you next time."
- "So glad the cake was a hit. Happy birthday to your sister!"

Vary your replies. If every comment gets the same line, it reads like a machine wrote it.

## Replying to questions

Questions in the comments are chances to help, and often to make a sale. Answer clearly, and add a next step.

| Question | Example reply |
|---|---|
| "Are you open on Sunday?" | "Yes, we are open on Sundays from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. See you then." |
| "How much is this?" | "It is $24. You can order in store or message us and we will set it aside." |
| "Do you deliver?" | "We do, within 5 miles, on weekdays. Send us a message with your address and we will confirm the fee." |
| "Is it gluten free?" | "This one is not, but we have a gluten-free option. Message us and we will tell you more." |

If you do not know the answer, say so and promise to find out. "Good question. Let us check and get back to you today" is better than a guess.

## Replying to reviews

Reviews and recommendations help other people decide. Reply to the good and the bad ones, and keep each reply short.

**For a positive review:** thank the person, mention one specific thing, and invite them back.

- "Thank you for the kind words, Daniel. We are glad the repair was quick. Come and see us any time."

**For a mixed review:** thank them for the honest feedback, own the part that is fair, and say what you will do.

- "Thank you for telling us. You are right that the wait was long. We have added a second person at the counter on Saturdays."

**For a negative review:** stay calm, apologize for the experience, and move it to a private conversation.

- "We are sorry your visit did not go well. That is not what we want for anyone. Please send us a message so we can look into what happened and make it right."

## Handling a complaint calmly

A complaint can sting. A clear process makes it easier.

1. **Pause before replying.** Wait until you are calm. Do not reply when you are angry.
2. **Read it twice.** Find what the person actually needs.
3. **Thank and acknowledge.** "Thank you for telling us" is a good start.
4. **Apologize for the experience,** even if you disagree on the facts. You can be sorry that they had a bad time without admitting fault you are not sure of.
5. **Offer a next step.** A private message, a phone call, a refund, or a repair.
6. **Do not argue in public.** You may disagree, but a long fight makes you look worse to everyone reading.
7. **Follow up.** If you promised something, do it.

Never reveal private details about a customer in a public reply, such as their order or their health. Keep it general and move the details to a private message.

## What not to do

- **Do not delete honest criticism.** Hiding a fair complaint breaks trust. Remove only content that is abusive, spam, or against the rules of the platform.
- **Do not write fake reviews** or ask friends to post praise that is not real. It is dishonest and can break platform rules.
- **Do not offer rewards for good reviews** unless you have checked Meta's current rules. Policies change, so read them first.
- **Do not copy and paste** the same reply to every review.
- **Do not get personal.** Reply to the issue, not the person.

## A simple routine that fits a busy day

- **Daily, five minutes:** check notifications, answer questions, and thank anyone who left a kind comment.
- **Weekly, fifteen minutes:** read all new reviews and replies. Note anything that comes up more than once.
- **Monthly, thirty minutes:** look at the most common questions. Turn the top three into posts, so people find the answer before they ask.

If you schedule posts ahead of time, tell your team who watches the comments when a post goes live. Our guide on [how to schedule Facebook Page posts](/blog/how-to-schedule-facebook-page-posts) explains how to set that up.

## Frequently asked questions

### How fast should I reply to a Facebook comment?

A day is a good aim for most small businesses. Faster is better for questions that block a sale. If you cannot check often, set expectations on your Page.

### Should I reply to every comment?

Not always. Reply to questions, complaints, and thoughtful comments. A like is enough for a simple emoji. Aim to answer anything that expects a response.

### What if the review is unfair?

Stay polite and factual. Say that you could not find a record, or explain what happened in one or two sentences, and offer to talk privately. Other readers will judge your tone.

### Can a tool reply for me?

A tool can suggest a draft, but customers can tell when a reply is generic. Always edit it to match the situation, and make sure it is true.

## Your next step

Open your Page and reply to the five most recent comments or reviews that have not had an answer. Write each one by hand, in your own voice. Then set a five-minute daily check on your calendar, and watch how much easier the habit becomes.

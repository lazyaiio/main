An online shop has a particular challenge on Facebook. Customers cannot touch the product, meet you, or see the shelves. Your Page has to do that work for you. That means showing the product clearly, answering the questions people have before they buy, and building enough trust that a stranger will send you money. This guide gives you thirty Facebook post ideas for online shops, plus a sample week and a few rules for staying honest and clear.

These ideas are our own suggestions, built from common shop routines. We cannot promise sales, and what works for one shop may not work for another. Post, read your numbers, and keep what earns messages and orders. For the method behind a full month of posts, see our guide on [how to plan a month of Facebook content](/blog/how-to-plan-a-month-of-facebook-content).

## What a shop Page is for

A shop Page has four jobs. It shows products in a way that a photo on a product page cannot, by adding people, stories, and context. It answers the questions that stop a sale, such as shipping time, returns, and sizing. It reminds past customers that you exist. And it sends people to the place where they can buy.

Because the goal is often a click or a message, every post should make the next step plain: where to buy, how to order, or how to ask a question.

## Thirty post ideas, grouped by goal

### Show the products (ideas 1 to 8)

1. **A product in use.** Show the item in a real setting, not only on a white background.
2. **A close-up of the detail.** Texture, stitching, or a label that shows quality.
3. **Size and scale.** Put the item next to a familiar object or a hand.
4. **A new arrival.** Name it, price it, and say where to buy it.
5. **A restock.** Tell people what is back in stock and how many are available, if you know.
6. **A bundle or set.** Show what is included and the price.
7. **A color or variant guide.** Show all options side by side.
8. **A gift idea.** Pick an item for a type of person, such as "for a new home."

### Show the people and the process (ideas 9 to 15)

9. **How it is made or chosen.** Show your process or your sourcing.
10. **Meet the maker or owner.** A photo and a short story of why you started.
11. **Packing day.** A short look at how you pack an order with care.
12. **Behind the scenes.** The studio, the stockroom, or the desk where you work.
13. **A supplier story.** Share where something comes from, with permission.
14. **A customer photo.** With permission, show a customer using your product and thank them.
15. **A team update.** A new hire, a milestone, or a day at an event.

### Answer buying questions (ideas 16 to 22)

16. **Shipping times and costs.** Be clear about what you charge and how long it usually takes.
17. **Returns and exchanges.** Explain the policy in plain words.
18. **Sizing or fit guide.** A simple chart or tip for choosing the right size.
19. **How to care for it.** Washing, charging, storage, or cleaning advice for the product.
20. **How to order.** Give the steps, or the link, or say you take orders by message.
21. **Payment options.** List what you accept.
22. **Holiday cutoffs.** Say the last order date for delivery before a holiday.

### Invite people to buy (ideas 23 to 30)

23. **A time-limited offer.** A real offer with exact dates and any limits.
24. **A new collection.** Show a few pieces and say when the full range arrives.
25. **A question post.** "Which color should we restock first?"
26. **A back-in-stock reminder.** Tell people to message you if they want a note when an item returns.
27. **A bestseller post.** Name your most popular product and say why customers choose it.
28. **A review spotlight.** Share a real review, with permission, and thank the customer.
29. **A giveaway.** Follow the rules in our guide to [Facebook giveaway rules for small businesses](/blog/facebook-giveaway-rules-for-small-businesses).
30. **A last-chance reminder.** "Our spring range ends on Sunday."

## A sample week you can copy

Here is an example week for an online shop that posts four times. Choose the days when your customers are most likely to shop.

| Day | Post type | Idea from the list |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | A product in use | 1 or 2 |
| Wednesday | A buying question answered | 16, 17, or 18 |
| Friday | A people or process post | 9 or 11 |
| Sunday | An offer or a question | 23 or 25 |

If four posts is too many, begin with two. Our guide on [how often to post on Facebook](/blog/how-often-should-you-post-on-facebook) can help you choose.

## Product photo tips

- **Use daylight.** Shoot near a window, and avoid harsh flash.
- **Show scale.** A coin or a hand next to the product helps.
- **Show the real item.** Color and size should match what you ship.
- **Add a lifestyle shot** for every plain product shot.
- **Keep the background simple** so the product stands out.
- **Write short alt text** in your own words if the tool lets you, so the image is usable for everyone.

## Writing tips for product posts

- **Put the key facts first:** what it is, the price, and where to buy.
- **Keep claims honest.** Do not promise results the product cannot deliver.
- **Show real prices.** If you cut a price, say what it was and when the old price applied. Check your local rules about price claims.
- **Add one clear next step,** such as a link or a message prompt.
- **Check the link** on a phone before you publish.

## Using messages to close the sale

Many online purchases start with a question. "Does it come in blue?" "Will it arrive by Friday?" Treat each message as a chance to help, and answer with facts. If a question comes up three times, turn it into a post, so the next customer finds the answer before they ask. Keep a short list of your most common questions and your best answers, and update it when your policies change.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Only posting products.** Mix in people, tips, and answers.
- **Hiding shipping costs.** Surprise fees lose orders and trust.
- **Using manufacturer photos only.** Customers want to see the real thing.
- **Forgetting to update stock.** A post for something sold out frustrates people.
- **Ignoring messages.** Many buyers ask a question before they order. Answer quickly.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should an online shop post?

Choose a rhythm you can keep. For many small shops that is two to four posts a week, plus quick replies to messages.

### Should I post a link in every post?

No. Posts that always send people away can feel pushy. Mix product posts that link out with tips, stories, and questions that keep the conversation on your Page.

### Can I use customer photos?

Yes, with their clear permission. Say how you will use the photo, and credit the customer only if they agree.

### Can I plan a month of shop posts at once?

Yes. Pick ideas that fit your range, give each a day, and write them in one go. Our [Facebook content calendar template](/blog/facebook-content-calendar-template) gives you a layout to start from.

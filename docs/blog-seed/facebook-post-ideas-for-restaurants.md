If you run a restaurant, a cafe, or a bakery, you already have more to post about than you think. Every day you cook, serve, greet, and clean up, and each of those moments can become a post. The hard part is not material. It is deciding which of it to share. This guide gives you thirty Facebook post ideas for restaurants that you can use this month, plus a sample week and a few rules that keep your Page useful and honest.

These ideas are our own suggestions, built from common restaurant routines. We cannot promise any result from them. The way to learn what works for your guests is to post, read the numbers, and keep what earns replies. For the method behind a full month of posts, see our guide on [how to plan a month of Facebook content](/blog/how-to-plan-a-month-of-facebook-content).

## What a restaurant Page is for

A restaurant Page has a few clear jobs. It tells people when you are open. It shows what the food looks like. It answers the questions guests ask all the time, such as "Do you take reservations?" and "Is there parking?" And it keeps you in mind between visits.

Because of that, your best posts are usually simple and local. A clear photo of a dish with the price and the hours does more work than a long speech about your passion for food. Keep the useful facts in every post, and let the personality show in how you write.

## Thirty post ideas, grouped by goal

### Show the food (ideas 1 to 8)

1. **Today's special.** One photo, the name of the dish, the price, and the hours it is available.
2. **A plate from above.** A tidy top-down photo of your most popular dish, taken near a window.
3. **A new dish.** Tell people what is in it and who it is for, such as "a mild curry for guests who do not like heat."
4. **The ingredient of the week.** Show one ingredient and the dish it appears in.
5. **A drink post.** Share a coffee, a mocktail, or a seasonal drink, with one line about what makes it special.
6. **A dessert reveal.** Show the whole dessert, then a cut slice in the next post.
7. **Menu changes.** Announce what is new, what is leaving, and for how long.
8. **A guest favorite.** Name the dish people order most and say why they love it, in your own words.

### Show the people (ideas 9 to 15)

9. **Meet the cook.** A short introduction with a photo, a favorite dish, and how long they have worked with you.
10. **A day in the kitchen.** Three or four photos from opening to first order.
11. **The supplier story.** Share where one ingredient comes from, with permission from the supplier.
12. **A team milestone.** A work anniversary or a new role, shared with the person's agreement.
13. **A guest moment.** A birthday or a family dinner, only with the guests' permission.
14. **Behind the counter.** Show how bread is shaped, a sauce is built, or a coffee is poured.
15. **A thank-you post.** Thank the regulars, the neighbors, or a local event that sent guests your way.

### Answer common questions (ideas 16 to 22)

16. **Opening hours.** Post them clearly, and post again whenever they change for a holiday.
17. **How to reserve.** Say whether you take bookings, how, and how far ahead.
18. **Where to park.** A photo of the entrance and a line about parking or the nearest stop.
19. **Allergy and diet notes.** Say what you can and cannot offer. Be careful and accurate, and tell guests to ask staff about their needs.
20. **Takeaway and delivery.** Explain how to order and what the usual wait is.
21. **Group bookings.** Say how large a group you can seat and how to ask.
22. **Gift cards or vouchers.** Say where to buy them and how they are used.

### Invite people in (ideas 23 to 30)

23. **A weekday offer.** A real, limited offer on a quiet day, with the exact dates.
24. **An event night.** Live music, a quiz, or a tasting, with the date and how to join.
25. **A seasonal menu.** Show the first dish and the date the full menu starts.
26. **A question post.** "Which should we add next: a lemon tart or a ginger cake?"
27. **A photo contest.** Follow the rules in our guide to [Facebook giveaway rules for small businesses](/blog/facebook-giveaway-rules-for-small-businesses) before you start.
28. **A local tie-in.** Join a town festival, a school event, or a market and say so.
29. **A review thank-you.** Share a kind comment from a guest, with their permission, and thank them.
30. **A reminder.** "Weekend bookings are open. Message us to hold a table."

## A sample week you can copy

A steady rhythm beats a burst of effort. Here is one example week for a small restaurant that posts four times. Change the days to match when your guests decide where to eat.

| Day | Post type | Idea from the list |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Hours and a weekday offer | 16 and 23 |
| Wednesday | A plate or a special | 1 or 2 |
| Friday | A people or behind-the-scenes post | 9 or 14 |
| Sunday | A question or a thank-you | 26 or 15 |

If four posts feels like too many, start with two. Our guide on [how often to post on Facebook](/blog/how-often-should-you-post-on-facebook) explains how to choose a rhythm you can keep.

## Photo and writing rules for food posts

- **Use natural light.** Sit the plate near a window and avoid the flash.
- **Show the real dish.** Guests should get what the photo shows.
- **Put the key fact in the first line.** Lead with the dish, the price, or the date.
- **Keep it short.** Most food posts need one to three sentences.
- **Add one clear next step.** Reserve, visit, message, or order.
- **Reply to every comment you can.** A quick answer to "Is it vegetarian?" can win a booking.

For help with openings and endings, read our guide to [hooks and calls to action](/blog/facebook-post-hooks-and-calls-to-action).

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Posting only discounts.** If every post is an offer, guests learn to wait for the next one.
- **Using photos that are not yours.** Stock food photos can mislead guests about what they will be served.
- **Forgetting to update hours.** Out-of-date hours cost you visits and trust.
- **Hiding the price.** If a dish is on the post, the price should be too.
- **Sharing guest photos without asking.** Always get permission first.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a restaurant post on Facebook?

Choose a number you can keep for months. For many small restaurants that is two to four posts a week. A steady, modest schedule is better than a busy week followed by silence.

### Should I post my full menu on Facebook?

A clear menu helps guests decide, so share it, and update it when it changes. Put the date on it, so nobody relies on an old version.

### What is the best type of post for a restaurant?

It depends on your guests. Photos of real dishes with clear facts are a good place to start. Check your Page's numbers after a month and do more of what gets replies, messages, and visits.

### Can I plan a whole month of restaurant posts in one sitting?

Yes. Pick the ideas above that fit your menu, assign them to days, and write them in a batch. Our free [content calendar generator](/tools/content-calendar-generator) can lay out the month for you.

You have a new product, and you want people to notice. The temptation is to post one big announcement and hope it works. A better approach is to write a Facebook post about a new product that answers the reader's real questions, and then to support it with a few follow-up posts across a week. This guide shows you how, step by step, with a simple structure, example posts, photo tips, and a launch-week plan.

The advice here is our own. It comes from common small-business practice, and it will not guarantee sales. Your customers know what they care about, so test and adjust. For the broader skill of writing posts that people read, see our guide on [how to write Facebook posts that people read](/blog/how-to-write-facebook-posts-people-read).

## What a reader wants to know

When someone sees a post about something new, they ask a few quick questions, even if they do not say them aloud.

1. What is it?
2. Who is it for?
3. Why is it different or useful?
4. How much is it?
5. Where and when can I get it?

If your post answers those five, it is doing its job. If it only says "We are excited to announce," it is not.

## A simple structure

Follow this shape for the main announcement.

1. **The first line.** Name the product and say what it does, in plain words.
2. **The reason.** Say who it helps and why, in one or two sentences.
3. **The facts.** Give the price, the sizes or options, and the date it is available.
4. **The step.** Say how to buy, order, or ask a question.

Keep it to a few short paragraphs. Add one strong photo. A reader who wants more can follow the link or ask in the comments.

## Example posts

These are examples, not real products.

**A bakery**

> Meet our new oat and seed loaf. It has a soft crust and a nutty flavor, and it stays fresh for three days. $5 a loaf, available from 8 a.m. from Thursday. Message us to reserve one.

**A skincare maker**

> Our new fragrance-free hand cream is here. It is made for people who wash their hands often, and it comes in a 50 ml tube. $12, in stock now. Tap the link to order, or ask us a question below.

**A repair shop**

> We now repair e-bikes. Bring in your bike for a free check, and we will give you a price before we start any work. Open Monday to Saturday. Send us a message to book a slot.

Each post names the product, says who it is for, gives the key facts, and ends with one step.

## Show the product, not only the box

The photo is half of the post. A few ways to make it work harder:

- **Show it in use.** A person using the product tells a story that a plain shot cannot.
- **Show the detail.** A close-up of a texture, a label, or a feature builds trust.
- **Show the size.** Put it next to something familiar.
- **Use natural light.** A window works better than flash.
- **Use real photos.** If you use supplier photos, make sure they match what you actually ship.

If you cannot take new photos, a short video of you handling the product can also help. Keep it plain and honest.

## Tell people why it matters to them

The most common mistake in product posts is talking only about the product. Readers care about what it does for them. Turn each feature into a benefit.

| Feature | Benefit for the reader |
|---|---|
| Stays fresh for three days | You can buy on Thursday and enjoy it through the weekend |
| Fragrance free | Suits people who prefer unscented products |
| Free check before repair | You know the price before you decide |

Use real benefits only. Do not claim health effects, savings, or results that you cannot support.

## A launch-week plan

One post is rarely enough. Here is an example plan for a new product. Adjust it to your business.

| Day | Post | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Main announcement | Tell people what it is and how to get it |
| Day 2 | Behind the scenes | Show how it is made or chosen |
| Day 4 | Close-up or how to use it | Answer practical questions |
| Day 5 | A question post | Ask which option people prefer |
| Day 7 | A customer moment or thank-you | Show early reactions, with permission |

Keep the posts different. Each should have a new first line, a new photo, and a new angle. Our guide on [how to repurpose content into Facebook posts](/blog/how-to-repurpose-content-into-facebook-posts) shows how to build several posts from one idea.

## Make the next step easy

Tell people exactly what to do. If you sell online, link straight to the product page, and check that it works on a phone. If you take orders by message, say what to include, such as the size or the color. If you sell in a shop, give the address and the hours. Our guide to [hooks and calls to action](/blog/facebook-post-hooks-and-calls-to-action) lists patterns that work without being pushy.

## Get the facts right before you write

Before you draft, collect the details in one place: the name, the price, the sizes or options, the date it goes on sale, how long delivery takes, and what the return rules are. Check each one with the person who knows best, such as your supplier or your team. A single wrong price in a launch post is hard to undo, because people screenshot it. If something is not final, leave it out and say it will follow.

## Answer questions after you post

A new product brings questions. Prices, sizes, delivery, and returns are common ones. Reply quickly and clearly. If the same question comes up several times, add the answer to your next post. Our guide to [replying to Facebook comments and reviews](/blog/how-to-reply-to-facebook-comments-and-reviews) has examples that you can adapt.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Only saying you are excited.** Say what the product is and does.
- **Leaving out the price.** Hidden prices make people hesitate or scroll away.
- **No date.** Tell people when it is available.
- **Overpromising.** Do not claim more than the product can do.
- **One post only.** Support the launch with several posts.
- **A broken link.** Test it on a phone.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a new product post be?

Three to five short sentences are usually enough. Put the name, the benefit, the price, and the next step in that order.

### Should I announce before the product is ready?

You can tease it, but be clear about what is available and when. If people cannot buy yet, say so, and tell them how to get notified.

### Do I need a video?

No. A clear photo and a good description are enough. A short video can help if the product is easier to understand in motion.

### Can I write the post with AI?

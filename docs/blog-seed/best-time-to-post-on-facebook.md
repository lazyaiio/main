Search for the best time to post on Facebook and you will find a dozen confident answers that do not agree with each other. That is not a coincidence. Advice about timing varies a lot from source to source because every Page has a different audience, in a different place, with different habits. A bakery's best time is not a law firm's best time.

So this guide does not give you a magic hour. It gives you a method to find your own, a test plan you can run in a month, and tips for audiences in different time zones. You will end up with an answer that is based on your Page.

## Why general advice fails

A published list of "best times" is an average of many Pages. Your Page is not average.

- **Your audience has its own routine.** Shift workers, students, and parents are online at different hours.
- **Your place matters.** Time zones and local habits change everything.
- **Your content matters.** A lunch special has a different best time than an evening class.
- **Things change.** People's habits, and Facebook, change over time.

Use general advice as a place to start a test, never as an answer.

## Start with what you know

Before you test, think about your customers.

1. **Where do they live?** Note their time zone or zones.
2. **When are they free?** Early morning, lunch, evening, or weekend?
3. **What are they doing when they read your posts?** Commuting, on a break, or relaxing at home?
4. **Which of your posts got a good response before?** Write down the day and time.

Your own answers give you a few good guesses to try. Pick two or three time slots, such as 8 AM, 12:30 PM, and 7 PM, to test.

## A simple test plan

Run this for four weeks. Keep everything else as steady as you can.

| Week | What to do |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | Post at your first time slot, three posts across the week |
| Week 2 | Post at your second time slot, three similar posts |
| Week 3 | Post at your third time slot, three similar posts |
| Week 4 | Repeat the best slot and compare |

Use similar kinds of posts in each week, such as the same mix of photos, tips, and offers. If one week has a big promotion and another does not, the test will mislead you.

## What to measure

Choose one or two numbers and use them for every post.

| Number | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| Reach | Shows how many people saw the post |
| Reactions and comments | Shows who responded |
| Link clicks | Shows who took the next step |

Write the numbers in a table with the day, the time, and the type of post. After a month you will see a pattern, or you will see that timing matters less than the post itself. ## If your audience is in several time zones

Many Pages serve people in more than one place. A time that works in Dhaka might be the middle of the night in New York. Plan with the time zones in front of you.

| Choice | When it works |
|---|---|
| Pick the time zone of your biggest group | Most of your customers live in one place |
| Alternate times | You have two main groups, so serve each in turn |
| Pick an overlap | Both groups are awake at the same time |

## Common mistakes

- **Trusting a general list.** It is an average of other Pages.
- **Testing too many things at once.** Change only the time.
- **Judging from one post.** One post proves little.
- **Ignoring time zones.** A good time for you may be a poor time for your readers.
- **Never testing again.** Habits change, so repeat the test now and then.
- **Forgetting the content.** A great post at an average time often beats a weak post at a perfect time.

## Read the results honestly

After four weeks you will have a table of posts and numbers. Take a few minutes to read it carefully before you decide anything. Start by sorting your posts by reach and by engagement, and look at the top three and the bottom three. Ask what they have in common. Is it the time, the day, the picture, or the topic?

Be cautious about small differences. If one time slot did only a little better than the others, it may be chance. A clear gap that repeats in different weeks is more convincing. If your results are mixed, that is also an answer: timing may matter less for your Page than the quality and the subject of the post. In that case, stop spending effort on the clock and spend it on better posts instead.

Write a one-sentence conclusion, such as "Weekday lunchtimes did a little better than evenings for offers." Put the date next to it. You are not stuck with this answer. It is simply the best guess you have today.

## Keep a posting log

A posting log turns a one-time test into a habit. After each post, add a row with the date, the time, the type of post, and the numbers a couple of days later. It takes less than a minute, and after three months you will have more useful information than most general articles can offer.

Review the log once a month. Look for patterns by day, by time, and by type of post. Then adjust your calendar for the next month. If you notice that your Sunday evening questions get more comments than your Monday morning tips, you can move more questions to Sunday. This is the real value of the method: a slow, steady improvement based on your own audience, not on someone else's average.

## What to do when the result is unclear

Sometimes a test ends without a clear winner, and that is fine. If every time slot looks about the same, you can relax. It probably means your followers see your posts when they open the app, and the exact minute matters little. In that case, choose the times that are convenient for you, keep a steady rhythm, and spend your energy on the content.

If your reach is low at every time, the problem may be the posts and not the schedule. Look at your first lines, your pictures, and the usefulness of what you share. ## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best time to post on Facebook?** It depends on your audience. The only reliable answer comes from testing your own Page.

**Does the day of the week matter?** It can. Add the day to your test notes and look for a pattern.

**How often should I repeat the test?** Every few months, or when your audience or your offer changes.

**Is it bad to post at odd hours?** Not necessarily. If your audience is awake then, it can work well.

**Should I post at the same time every day?** A steady rhythm helps people expect you, but vary the time slightly if your tests show a benefit.

Posting every day on the spot takes a surprising amount of energy. Each time you open Facebook, you have to decide what to say, find a photo, write the text, and press publish. The better way is to do this work in one block. This guide shows you how to schedule a month of Facebook posts in about an hour, with a time budget, a sample plan, and a short weekly check.

An honest note: one hour works best when you already have photos and know your business well. Your first month may take longer, and that is fine. The routine gets faster each time. For the bigger planning method behind it, see our guide on [how to plan a month of Facebook content](/blog/how-to-plan-a-month-of-facebook-content).

## Why batching works

- **You switch tasks less.** Writing twelve posts in a row is faster than writing one post twelve times.
- **You write better.** With the whole month in view, you can balance tips, stories, and offers.
- **You stay steady.** The Page keeps posting even in a busy week.
- **You can still change things.** Scheduled posts can be edited or replaced when news comes up.

A steady rhythm also needs a realistic number of posts. Our guide on [how often to post on Facebook](/blog/how-often-should-you-post-on-facebook) will help you choose.

## What you need before you start

Gather these before you begin, so the hour is spent on writing, not hunting:

1. **A posting rhythm.** For example, three posts a week, which is about twelve a month.
2. **A list of dates.** Holidays, sales, events, and closures for the month.
3. **A folder of photos.** Aim for at least as many as you have posts.
4. **A few facts.** Prices, opening hours, and offers that must be accurate.
5. **Your scheduling tool,** logged in and connected to your Page.

## The one-hour time budget

This is a suggested budget based on our own routine, not a rule. Adjust it to fit.

| Step | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Set the rhythm and mark the dates | 5 minutes | Choose post days and list special dates |
| 2. Choose the post types | 10 minutes | Match a type to each slot |
| 3. Write the posts | 25 minutes | Draft all posts in one go |
| 4. Add photos and links | 10 minutes | Attach one image to each post |
| 5. Schedule | 10 minutes | Set date and time for each |

Set a timer for each step. If a step runs over, move on and come back later.

## Step 1: Set the rhythm and mark the dates

Decide how many posts you will publish and on which days. Write the special dates first, since those posts are fixed: a sale, an event, a holiday closure. Everything else fills the gaps. The free [content calendar generator](/tools/content-calendar-generator) can lay out a month for you in seconds.

## Step 2: Choose the post types

Mix the types so the Page does not become a stream of offers. A balanced month for a small business might look like this, as an example:

- **About four tips or how-to posts.** They help and build trust.
- **About three behind-the-scenes or people posts.** They show who you are.
- **About two question or conversation posts.** They invite replies.
- **About two offers or announcements.** They ask for the sale.
- **About one customer story or thank-you.** It shows proof, with permission.

That adds up to twelve. Change the mix to match your goals. For a ready layout, see our [Facebook content calendar template](/blog/facebook-content-calendar-template).

## Step 3: Write the posts

Work through the list in order and write a quick draft of each. Do not stop to polish. Keep each post to one idea and one next step. If you get stuck, use a template from our list of [Facebook post templates for small business](/blog/facebook-post-templates-for-small-business), or take a prompt from the guide to [AI prompts for Facebook posts](/blog/ai-prompts-for-facebook-posts).

If you use an AI tool, give it the facts, ask for a short draft, and edit every one. Do not publish what you have not read.

## Step 4: Add photos and links

Attach one clear photo to each post when it helps. Use your own photos where you can. If you need stock images, check the license and pick pictures that fit your business honestly. If a post includes a link, open it and make sure it works on a phone.

## Step 5: Schedule

Set the date and time for each post. A few practical points:

- **Check the time zone** in your tool before you start.
- **Choose times when your audience is likely online,** and test them. Our guide to the [best time to post on Facebook](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-facebook) explains how to find yours.
- **Leave gaps.** Do not fill every single day if your plan says three posts a week.
- **Keep a few slots open** for news, such as a new product or a local event.

If your audience is in another time zone, the free [time zone converter](/tools/time-zone-converter) helps you pick the right hour.

## The two-minute weekly check

Scheduling ahead does not mean forgetting. Once a week, take two minutes to check:

1. Did last week's posts go out?
2. Is anything coming up that needs to change, such as a closed date or a sold-out item?
3. Are there comments or messages that need a reply?

If a post fails, our guide on [why a scheduled Facebook post fails](/blog/why-a-scheduled-facebook-post-failed) lists the common causes and fixes.

## A sample month

This is an example for a small garden shop that posts three times a week. Dates are illustrative.

| Week | Monday | Wednesday | Friday |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tip: watering in hot weather | Behind the scenes: new stock arrives | Question: your favorite herb? |
| 2 | Tip: pruning basics | Offer: seed packs this weekend | Customer story: a balcony garden |
| 3 | Plant of the week | Behind the scenes: potting up | Question: balcony or backyard? |
| 4 | Tip: composting at home | Announcement: new opening hours | Thank-you: for a great month |

Notice the pattern: a tip, a people post, and a conversation post each week, with one offer in the month.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Scheduling without reading.** Check every post once before it goes live.
- **Filling the whole month with offers.** Mix in helpful and human posts.
- **Ignoring comments.** Scheduled posts still need replies.
- **Scheduling too far ahead without a check-in.** A month is fine if you review weekly. Offers and prices change.
- **Forgetting special dates.** Mark holidays and closures first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a month too far ahead?

For most Pages it is fine, if you check each week. If your business changes quickly, such as a daily menu, schedule two weeks at a time instead.

### Can I edit scheduled posts?

Most tools let you edit or delete a scheduled post before it goes out. Check how yours works.

### What if I only have time for half an hour?

Schedule two weeks instead of four. A smaller batch done regularly beats a large one you skip.

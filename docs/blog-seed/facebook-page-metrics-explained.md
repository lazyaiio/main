Open your Page's performance screen and you will see a wall of numbers. Views, reach, engagement, followers, clicks. It is easy to feel busy and still learn nothing. This guide explains the main Facebook Page metrics in plain words, shows which ones matter for a small business, and gives you a simple monthly routine.

The names Facebook uses can change, and some numbers have been retired or renamed over the years. So treat the exact labels on your screen as the final word, and use this guide to understand the ideas behind them.

## Start with the question, not the numbers

A number only helps if it answers a question. Before you look at anything, decide what you want to know. Here are three useful questions.

- **Are more people seeing my posts?** This is about reach and views.
- **Do they care about what they see?** This is about engagement.
- **Is my audience growing?** This is about followers.

Everything else is a detail. You can answer most questions about a small Page with just these three groups of numbers.

## Views: how often your content was shown

Views count how many times your content was played or displayed. That includes your posts, videos, stories, and ads. One person can create many views, because they might see the same post several times.

Views are useful as a general sign of activity. When they rise, your content is being shown more often. They do not tell you whether people liked it, and they do not tell you how many different people saw it. For that you need the next number.

## Reach: how many different people saw it

Reach counts the number of different people who saw your content. It is closer to the question "how many people did I actually reach?" than views are. Facebook describes some of these counts as estimates, so do not worry about small differences.

If your views are high and your reach is low, the same small group is seeing your posts again and again. If both rise together, you are reaching new people. Comparing the two can tell you something views alone cannot.

## Engagement: what people did

Engagement counts the actions people take on your posts. Facebook includes reactions, comments, shares, and other actions in this group. It shows that people did more than scroll past.

Not all engagement is equal. A comment or a share usually takes more effort than a reaction, so a post with many comments may have started a real conversation. Read the comments themselves, not only the totals.

To compare posts or months fairly, turn engagement into a rate. Our [engagement rate calculator](/tools/engagement-rate-calculator) shows two common ways to do it: by followers, and by reach. Use the same way every time so your numbers stay comparable.

## Followers: is your audience growing

Follower numbers show how many people chose to follow your Page. The raw total matters less than the change. Ask how many new followers you gained this month, and whether you lost any.

A rising count is good, but check whether the new followers fit your customers. Fifty followers from the other side of the world may do nothing for a local shop. Our [follower growth calculator](/tools/follower-growth-calculator) turns two counts into a growth rate and an average per week, so you can compare months.

## Other numbers you may see

- **Page visits.** How many times people viewed your Page's profile. It can show interest in your business itself, not just one post.
- **Clicks.** How often people clicked your links or buttons. This is where views turn into action.
- **Contact and button clicks.** How often people used your phone number, directions, or call-to-action button.

For a business that wants calls or visits, these last numbers can matter more than reactions. If you share links in your posts, you can follow what happens next on your website with tagged links. ## Which three numbers to watch each month

Keep it small. Pick one number from each group.

1. **Reach,** to see whether more different people see your posts.
2. **Engagement rate,** to see whether they respond.
3. **New followers,** to see whether your audience grows.

If you have a specific goal, add one more. A restaurant might add direction clicks. An online shop might add link clicks.

## A simple monthly routine

You can finish this in twenty minutes once a month.

1. **Pick the same period each time,** such as the last 28 days.
2. **Write down your three numbers** in a note or spreadsheet, with the date.
3. **Compare with the month before.** Is each number up, down, or flat?
4. **Find your best and worst posts.** What did the best ones have in common? A photo, a question, a time of day?
5. **Choose one change** to try next month, and write it down.

This habit builds your own picture of what works for your Page. That is more useful than any general rule. It also fits into your monthly plan, which our guide on [how to plan a month of Facebook content](/blog/how-to-plan-a-month-of-facebook-content) explains step by step.

## A worked example

Here is how a small bakery might read one month. Last month the Page had 3,000 views from 800 people reached, 96 engagements, and 12 new followers. This month it has 3,600 views from 1,000 people reached, 130 engagements, and 15 new followers.

Reach rose by a quarter, so more different people saw the posts. Views rose by a fifth, a bit less than reach, so people did not see the same posts as many times each. Engagements rose by more than a third, so the posts also got more responses. New followers rose a little. The bakery can see that the month went well, and it can look at which posts brought the extra engagement.

Notice what the bakery did not do. It did not compare itself with another Page, and it did not worry about one slow day. It read three numbers against its own last month and asked what changed.

## How to read the ups and downs

Some movement is normal. A holiday, a local event, or the weather can change how people use Facebook, and none of it has anything to do with your posts. Before you decide something is wrong, look at four or five weeks side by side.

Ask these questions when a number moves a lot.

- **Did I post more or less than usual?** Fewer posts often means fewer views.
- **Did one post do something different?** A single popular post can lift the whole month.
- **Did I change the time or the format?** Photos, videos, and text-only posts can behave differently.
- **Is the period the same length?** A 31-day month will beat a 28-day one on raw totals.

Write your answers in your notes. After a few months you will see patterns that are specific to your Page.

## Common mistakes

- **Chasing one number.** Reach without engagement, or followers without sales, tells half the story.
- **Comparing with other Pages.** Pages differ in size, topic, and audience. Compare with your own past.
- **Judging by a single post.** One strong or weak post says little. Look at several weeks.
- **Reading too much into small changes.** Numbers move up and down by chance, and some are estimates.
- **Ignoring the delay.** Facebook updates many numbers about once a day, and recent days can still change.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a good engagement rate?** It depends on your Page size, your topic, and how you calculate it. Compare with your own earlier results rather than a general average.

**Why do my numbers differ from another tool?** Different tools may use different definitions, time periods, or time zones. Compare numbers from the same place and period.

**Why did my reach drop?** Many things can cause it, from the time you posted to what you posted. Look at several weeks before you change your plan.

**Do I need a paid tool?** Not to start. Facebook's own Page tools give you the main numbers. A tool can help later if you manage several Pages or want everything in one place.

## Your next step

Open your Page's performance screen this week and write down three numbers: reach, engagement, and new followers for the last 28 days. Put the date next to them. In a month, do it again and compare. That small habit will teach you more than any long report.

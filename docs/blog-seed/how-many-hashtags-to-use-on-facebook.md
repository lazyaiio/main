Hashtags can feel confusing on Facebook. Some people add ten to every post. Others add none. So how many hashtags to use on Facebook is a fair question, and the honest answer is that there is no single number that works for every Page. What you can do is start with a sensible habit, keep your tags tidy, and learn from your own results.

This guide gives you a starting point, explains where hashtags go, shows how to format them so everyone can read them, and gives you a simple way to test. Our advice here comes from experience and common sense, not from a rule published by Facebook, and we say so where it matters.

## What a hashtag does

A hashtag is a word or phrase with a # sign in front of it. It turns the words into a link that groups posts about the same topic. People can tap a hashtag to see other posts that use it.

For a small business Page, hashtags usually do two jobs.

- **They label your topic.** A tag such as #SourdoughBread tells readers and Facebook what the post is about.
- **They build a brand habit.** A tag you use every time, such as your business name, becomes a signature.

They are not magic. A good post with no hashtags can still do well, and a poor post with ten hashtags will still be a poor post.

## How many to start with

Our suggestion is to start small. Two or three relevant hashtags are plenty for most posts. Here is why.

| Number | What usually happens |
|---|---|
| None | Fine for a simple update. The post still works |
| 1 to 3 | Easy to read, and each tag has a clear job |
| 4 to 6 | Starts to look crowded, and some tags are weaker |
| 7 or more | Often looks like spam and distracts from your message |

This table is advice, not a measured result. Your audience may behave differently, so treat it as a starting point and run the test below.

## Where to put them

Put hashtags at the end of the post, after your message and call to action. That keeps your first line clean and your message easy to read. A single line of two or three tags looks neat.

You can also place one tag inside a sentence when it reads naturally, such as "Our #SpringSale starts Friday." Do not scatter tags through every line.

## How to format hashtags

Good formatting makes tags easier to read and use.

| Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| #FreshBread | #freshbread | Capital letters mark where each word starts, so it is easier to read |
| #SpringSale2027 | #spring sale 2027 | A space ends the tag, so only the first word becomes a link |
| #Bakery | #Bakery! | Punctuation does not belong inside the tag |
| #MyShopName | #my-shop-name | Hyphens and symbols break the tag |
| #CafeDhaka | #123 | A tag made only of numbers is not useful |

Capitalizing each word, often called camel case, also helps people who use screen readers, because the words can be read separately. If you paste a messy list of keywords, our free [hashtag cleaner](/tools/hashtag-cleaner) fixes spaces, duplicates, and punctuation in one step.

## Choose the right tags

A good tag matches what the post is really about. Here are three kinds to mix.

- **Your own tag.** Your business name or a short slogan, used on every post.
- **A topic tag.** The subject of the post, such as #SourdoughBread.
- **A local tag.** Your town or neighborhood, if you serve a local area.

Avoid tags that have nothing to do with your post. They do not help, and they can make your Page look careless. Avoid very broad tags too. A tag with millions of posts is crowded, so yours will be lost.

## Test what works for your Page

You do not have to guess. Run a simple test for a month.

1. **Choose a baseline.** For two weeks, use your normal number of tags and note the results.
2. **Change one thing.** For the next two weeks, use two or three tags only.
3. **Compare.** Look at reach and engagement for the two periods. Our [engagement rate calculator](/tools/engagement-rate-calculator) makes this easy.
4. **Decide.** Keep the approach that worked better for your Page.
5. **Repeat later.** Audiences change, so test again every few months.

Be careful about what you conclude. Many things change between two weeks, such as the topic, the photo, and the day of the week. A small test suggests an answer. It does not prove one.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Stuffing in tags.** More tags do not mean more reach.
- **Using the same long list every time.** It looks automatic.
- **Hiding the message under tags.** Put your point first.
- **Using spaces inside a tag.** Only the first word will link.
- **Borrowing unrelated trending tags.** It can look like spam and may annoy people.
- **Forgetting accessibility.** Capitalize each word in long tags.

## Hashtags for a small local business

If you serve a town or a neighborhood, hashtags work a little differently. Your goal is not to reach the whole world. It is to be found by the people nearby who might become customers.

Think about how a neighbor would describe your business. They might say the name of your area, the kind of shop you run, and the thing they are looking for. Those words make good tags, such as the name of your neighborhood followed by the word bakery. A local tag is crowded by fewer posts, so yours has a better chance of being seen by the right people.

It also helps to keep a small family of tags that you reuse. Choose one tag for your business name, one for your area, and one for your main product. Then add a topic tag that fits each post. Over time your own tags collect your posts in one place, and a new visitor can tap one to see what you have been up to. That gives your hashtags a real job, instead of leaving them as decoration at the end of a post.

## Hashtags and accessibility

Hashtags should be easy for everyone to read, including people who use a screen reader. A tag in all lowercase letters, such as freshbreadtoday, can be read out as one long confusing word. A tag with capital letters, such as FreshBreadToday, lets the software see where each word begins, so it can read them separately.

Keep your tags short, and put them at the end of the post so that the main message is read first. Avoid long strings of tags and avoid emojis inside a tag. These small habits cost you nothing and make your posts welcoming to more readers.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do hashtags work on Facebook?** They can group your post with others on the same topic, but they matter less than the quality of the post. Test them on your own Page.

**Can I use the same hashtags on every post?** You can use one or two as your brand tags. Vary the topic tag so it fits each post.

**Should I put hashtags in a comment?** Some people do. It keeps the post clean, but the tags may be seen less. Try it and compare.

**Are capital letters important?** The tag works either way, but capitals make long tags much easier to read.

**Is there an official number?** We are not aware of a published rule. Read Facebook's current guidance, and rely on your own results.

## Your next step

Look at your last five posts. Count the hashtags, fix the formatting, and trim each post to two or three relevant tags. Then watch your results for two weeks and compare.

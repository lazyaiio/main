A photo catches the eye, but a caption does the talking. It tells people what they are looking at, why it matters, and what to do next. Many small businesses post a good photo and then leave the words as an afterthought. That wastes the most valuable moment, when someone has stopped scrolling. This guide shows you how to write Facebook captions for photos that add context, sound like you, and invite a reply, with a simple formula and 25 examples to adapt.

The advice here is our own and comes from common writing practice, not from Facebook. Your audience will tell you what works, so test and adjust. For the broader skill of writing posts, see our guide on [how to write Facebook posts that people read](/blog/how-to-write-facebook-posts-people-read).

## What a caption should do

A good caption does one or more of these jobs:

- **Explain.** Say what the photo shows, in a few words.
- **Add what the picture cannot show.** The price, the place, the story, the date.
- **Invite a response.** A question or a next step.
- **Sound human.** A caption is a voice, not a label.

If the caption only repeats the picture, such as "A cake," it adds nothing. If it adds one useful or warm detail, it earns its place.

## A simple caption formula

Use four short parts, in this order. You do not need all four every time.

1. **The hook.** One line that makes sense on its own.
2. **The detail.** A fact the photo cannot show.
3. **The feeling or story.** A short human touch.
4. **The step.** One thing to do next.

**Example:** "Our lemon tart is back. It is made with fruit from a farm ten minutes away. Baked fresh each morning, and gone by lunch most days. Message us to set one aside."

## Match the caption to the photo

Different photos need different captions.

| Photo | What to say |
|---|---|
| A product | Name, price, and where to buy |
| A person | Who they are and one thing they do |
| A place or event | Where, when, and why to come |
| A before and after | What changed, with the client's permission |
| A customer moment | A thank-you, with permission |
| A behind-the-scenes shot | What is happening and why it matters |

## Twenty-five caption examples

These are examples, not real posts. Replace the details with your own.

### For products (1 to 5)

1. "New in: hand-poured candles in three scents. $14 each. Message us to order."
2. "Small batch, big flavor. Our chili oil is back in stock."
3. "Made to last. Look closely at the stitching on this bag."
4. "Same recipe since the day we opened. Come and taste it."
5. "Which would you pick: the blue or the green?"

### For people and the team (6 to 10)

6. "Meet Ana. She has been baking our rolls since 5 a.m. for six years."
7. "Our newest team member, Jo, starts today. Say hello."
8. "Thirty years in the trade, and still loves the first cut of the day."
9. "The people behind the counter. Come and meet them."
10. "A team photo after a busy Saturday. Thank you for coming."

### For places and events (11 to 15)

11. "Our garden is open from 10 a.m. Bring a friend and a book."
12. "Set up and ready for tonight's class. A few spots are left."
13. "The view from our front door this morning."
14. "Our stall at the market is here until 3 p.m. Come and say hello."
15. "The shop has had a fresh coat of paint. Come and see."

### For stories and moments (16 to 20)

16. "A customer brought this chair back after thirty years. We gave it a new life."
17. "Before: a cracked screen. After: back in a pocket in thirty minutes."
18. "A small thank-you from us to everyone who came to the open day."
19. "This is what a busy morning looks like. Coffee first."
20. "We got this wrong last month. Here is what we changed."

### For questions and conversation (21 to 25)

21. "Tea or coffee on a rainy day? Tell us below."
22. "What should we bake next: lemon or ginger?"
23. "Spot the detail. What did you notice first?"
24. "Caption this photo. Our favorite gets a free coffee." (Check platform rules for contests before you run one.)
25. "Where should we hold our next pop-up? Share your ideas."

## Writing tips that make captions better

- **Put the key point first.** Facebook usually shows only the start of a long caption before a "See more" link.
- **Use plain words.** Say "help" rather than "empower."
- **Keep one idea per caption.** If you have two, make two posts.
- **Add a detail only you know.** A name, a number, or a small joke.
- **Read it aloud.** If you would not say it, change it.
- **Keep emoji light.** Use a few if they fit your voice, or none.
- **Check the facts.** Prices, dates, and names must be right.

## Captions, hashtags, and tags

Hashtags on Facebook are optional, and a few relevant ones are enough. Our guide on [how many hashtags to use on Facebook](/blog/how-many-hashtags-to-use-on-facebook) explains how to decide. Tag people or businesses only when they have agreed, and when it helps the reader.

## Privacy and permission

A photo of a person is also about that person. Before you post a photo of a customer, a child, or a staff member, ask them. If the photo shows a child, ask a parent or guardian. If someone asks you to remove a photo, do it quickly. Follow the privacy rules in your area, since they differ.

## Editing a caption in three passes

Write the first version quickly, then edit it. In the first pass, cut anything that repeats the photo. In the second, check that the most important fact comes first, whether that is the price, the date, or the question. In the third, read it aloud and change any phrase that does not sound like you. A caption that has been through these three passes is usually shorter, clearer, and warmer than the first draft. If you write many captions at once, do the first pass for all of them, then the second, then the third. Batching the edits keeps your attention on one task at a time.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Writing nothing.** A photo alone gives people no next step.
- **Repeating the picture.** "A nice sunset" adds no value.
- **Hiding key facts in a long caption.** Put the price or date early.
- **Using the same caption again and again.** Readers notice.
- **Overusing exclamation marks and capital letters.** They feel like shouting.
- **Tagging people without asking.** It can upset them.
- **Posting a blurry photo.** A clear picture is half the post.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a photo caption be?

One to three sentences suit most photo posts. A story photo can have a few short paragraphs.

### Should every photo have a call to action?

Not always. A photo can simply share a moment. Over a month, make sure that most posts give people something to do.

### Can AI write my captions?

A tool can give you a draft. Edit it so that it sounds like you and is accurate. Our list of [AI prompts for Facebook posts](/blog/ai-prompts-for-facebook-posts) shows how to ask for a useful first version.

### What about people who cannot see the photo?

Some readers use screen readers. Where your tool allows it, add a short description of the image. Alt text is a separate field from the caption.

## Your next step

Take your last five photo posts and rewrite the captions with the formula: hook, detail, feeling, step. Compare them with the originals, and keep the habit that feels most natural.

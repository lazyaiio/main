You post, people react, and you wonder if it is going well. Raw counts do not answer that, because a Page with 5,000 followers and a Page with 500 followers cannot be compared by likes alone. To fairly calculate engagement rate on Facebook, you turn the counts into a percentage. This guide shows the two common formulas, works through examples, and explains how to use the result without fooling yourself.

You do not need a spreadsheet. You need a few numbers from your Page's performance screen and about five minutes. Or you can skip the math and use our free [engagement rate calculator](/tools/engagement-rate-calculator).

## What engagement rate means

Engagement rate is the share of people who did something with your content. "Did something" usually means reacting, commenting, sharing, or clicking. The rate answers a simple question: out of the people who could have responded, how many did?

The hard part is that there is more than one answer, because there is more than one way to decide who "could have responded." That is why you will see two formulas.

## The two formulas

[[diagram: funnel]]

| Formula | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Rate by followers | Average engagements per post, divided by followers, times 100 | Comparing your own months, and a quick check on audience size |
| Rate by reach | Total engagements, divided by total reach, times 100 | Judging how interesting your posts were to the people who actually saw them |

Both use the same list of engagements. Here is what to count.

- **Reactions** on your posts
- **Comments** on your posts
- **Shares** of your posts
- **Link clicks,** if you want to count them

Decide once what you count and keep it the same every month. A rate is only useful when you compare it with the same method.

## Formula 1: rate by followers

Use this when you want to know how much of your audience responds to a typical post.

1. Add up all engagements for the period.
2. Divide by the number of posts. This is the average per post.
3. Divide by your follower count.
4. Multiply by 100.

Here is an example. A bakery published 8 posts in a month and its Page has 1,200 followers. Across those posts it received 320 reactions, 64 comments, and 28 shares, which is 412 engagements.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Total engagements | 320 + 64 + 28 | 412 |
| Average per post | 412 divided by 8 | 51.5 |
| Divide by followers | 51.5 divided by 1,200 | 0.0429 |
| Rate by followers | 0.0429 times 100 | 4.29% |

So a typical post got a response from about 4.3 out of every 100 followers.

## Formula 2: rate by reach

Use this when you want to know how interesting your posts were to the people who really saw them. Followers do not always see every post, so reach can be a fairer base.

1. Add up all engagements for the period.
2. Divide by total reach for the same period.
3. Multiply by 100.

Using the same bakery, suppose its posts reached 9,000 people in total. With 412 engagements, the rate by reach is 412 divided by 9,000, times 100, which is 4.58%.

## Which formula should you use

| Your question | Use |
|---|---|
| Is my audience responding more than last month? | Rate by followers |
| Are my posts good for the people who see them? | Rate by reach |
| I want one number to track every month | Pick one and never change it |
| I am comparing two posts | Rate by reach for each post |

If you can only choose one, choose the formula that matches the question you ask most often, and write down how you calculated it.

## How to use the number well

An engagement rate is a tool for comparing you with yourself. Here are habits that keep it honest.

- **Compare the same period.** Use 28 days against 28 days, not a short month against a long one.
- **Do not chase other Pages.** Audiences, topics, and sizes differ too much for an average to mean much.
- **Look at the posts behind the number.** Find your best and worst posts and ask what was different.
- **Watch small Pages carefully.** A Page with 100 followers can swing wildly from a few reactions.
- **Combine it with other goals.** A high rate with no visits or sales may not help your business.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing methods.** Last month by followers and this month by reach tells you nothing.
- **Counting differently each time.** If you add clicks one month and skip them the next, the rate moves for no real reason.
- **Using a different follower count.** Use the count at the same point each time, such as the end of the period.
- **Judging from one post.** One viral or flat post can distort a month.
- **Ignoring the delay.** Facebook updates many numbers about once a day, and recent days can still change.

## A simple monthly routine

1. Pick your period, such as the last 28 days.
2. Write down total reactions, comments, shares, and clicks.
3. Write down your posts, your followers, and your reach.
4. Calculate the rate by followers and the rate by reach.
5. Save the numbers with the date, and compare with last month.
6. Choose one thing to try next month.

This takes about ten minutes a month, and it builds a record that no general benchmark can match. It also fits a monthly plan, which our guide to [planning a month of Facebook content](/blog/how-to-plan-a-month-of-facebook-content) explains.

## A worked month, step by step

Seeing the whole routine once makes it easy to repeat. Imagine a small yoga studio that keeps a note each month. In October it published 10 posts. Its Page had 2,000 followers on the last day of the month. The posts earned 410 reactions, 95 comments, and 45 shares, and reached 12,500 people.

First it adds the engagements: 410 plus 95 plus 45 is 550. For the follower formula it divides 550 by 10 posts to get 55 per post, divides that by 2,000 followers, and multiplies by 100. The result is 2.75 percent. For the reach formula it divides 550 by 12,500 and multiplies by 100, which is 4.4 percent.

In November the studio repeats the same steps. If November shows 3.1 percent by followers and 4.0 percent by reach, the story is interesting. More followers responded, but the posts were a little less engaging to the people who saw them. That is a reason to look at the posts, not a reason to panic. Perhaps a few posts reached many people who did not care much. The numbers do not give the answer, but they tell you where to look.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a good engagement rate on Facebook?** It depends on your Page size, your topic, and how you calculate it. Compare with your own earlier results instead of a general average.

**Should I count link clicks as engagement?** You can, as long as you count them every month. Some people leave them out to focus on reactions, comments, and shares.

**Is a higher rate always better?** Not always. A rate can be high because few people saw the posts. Look at reach and results such as messages or visits too.

**How often should I calculate it?** Once a month is enough for most small Pages. Calculating daily mostly measures noise.

**Do I need a tool?** No. The math is simple, and the calculator above does it for you in a moment.

Staring at an empty box is the slowest way to write a post. A template gives you a shape to fill, so you spend your time on the part only you can do: the details of your business. These Facebook post templates for small business cover the twelve situations that come up most often. Each has blanks to fill, a short example, and a note on when to use it.

Templates are a starting point, not a script. If every post sounds the same, readers will notice. Use these to get a clean first draft, then change the words until they sound like you. For the full approach to writing posts people want to read, see our guide on [how to write Facebook posts that people read](/blog/how-to-write-facebook-posts-people-read).

## How to use these templates

1. Pick the template that matches your goal for the post.
2. Fill in every blank with real details: names, prices, dates, and places.
3. Read it aloud. Change any line that you would not say to a customer.
4. Add one clear next step if the template does not already have one.
5. Check the first line. It should make sense on its own.

Words in capital letters are the blanks. Replace them with your own.

## Template 1: The helpful tip

**Use it to:** show that you know your field.

> Quick tip: HOW TO DO A USEFUL THING. It works because REASON. Try it this week and tell us how it goes.

**Example:** "Quick tip: wash new jeans inside out in cold water. It works because it keeps the color from fading. Try it this week and tell us how it goes."

## Template 2: The new arrival

**Use it to:** announce a product, dish, or service.

> NEW THING is here. It is made for PERSON WHO NEEDS IT, and it includes FEATURE. Price: PRICE. Available: WHERE OR WHEN.

**Example:** "Our new oat loaf is here. It is made for anyone who likes a softer crust, and it includes seeds on top. Price: $5. Available: from 8 a.m. daily."

## Template 3: The question

**Use it to:** start a conversation.

> QUESTION ABOUT A CHOICE YOUR CUSTOMERS MAKE? Tell us in the comments. We will share the most popular answer on DAY.

**Example:** "Which is your go-to for a rainy day: a hot chocolate or a strong coffee? Tell us in the comments. We will share the most popular answer on Friday."

## Template 4: The behind-the-scenes

**Use it to:** show the people and the work.

> This morning, PERSON was doing TASK. It takes TIME OR STEPS, and the part we love is SMALL DETAIL. Come and see it at PLACE.

**Example:** "This morning, Sara was hand-painting our new sign. It takes about three hours, and the part we love is the tiny gold edge. Come and see it at our front door."

## Template 5: The customer story

**Use it to:** build trust, with the customer's permission.

> A customer came in with PROBLEM. We did SOLUTION, and now THE RESULT. If you have a similar problem, MESSAGE US OR VISIT.

**Example:** "A customer came in with a cracked phone screen and a trip the next day. We replaced it in 30 minutes, and now it is good as new. If you have a similar problem, send us a message."

## Template 6: The offer

**Use it to:** invite sales honestly.

> OFFER DETAILS from START DATE to END DATE. It applies to WHAT IT COVERS. To claim it, HOW TO CLAIM.

**Example:** "Ten percent off all plants from Monday to Sunday. It applies to anything in the garden section. To claim it, show this post at the till."

Always state the real end date and any limits. Our guide to [hooks and calls to action](/blog/facebook-post-hooks-and-calls-to-action) explains how to ask without pressure.

## Template 7: The event

**Use it to:** get people to show up.

> Join us on DATE at TIME for EVENT. It is at PLACE, and it is FREE OR PRICE. To save a spot, HOW TO RESERVE.

**Example:** "Join us on Saturday at 10 a.m. for a candle-making class. It is at our studio on Main Street, and it costs $20. To save a spot, send us a message."

## Template 8: The myth buster

**Use it to:** correct a common belief.

> Myth: COMMON BELIEF. The truth: WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE. Here is why: SHORT REASON.

**Example:** "Myth: you must water a cactus every day. The truth: most cacti need water about every two weeks. Here is why: their roots rot in wet soil."

Make sure the claim is true and that you can explain it.

## Template 9: The thank-you

**Use it to:** show gratitude and warmth.

> Thank you to WHO for WHAT THEY DID. It meant THE EFFECT, and we are grateful. See you at NEXT EVENT OR VISIT.

**Example:** "Thank you to everyone who came to our open day. It meant a full house and a lot of smiles, and we are grateful. See you at our next class in March."

## Template 10: The reminder

**Use it to:** nudge people before a deadline.

> A reminder: THING happens on DATE. You can still HOW TO TAKE PART. After DATE, WHAT CHANGES.

**Example:** "A reminder: our spring sale ends on Sunday. You can still shop in store or message us to order. After Sunday, prices go back to normal."

## Template 11: The how-it-works

**Use it to:** explain a service in plain words.

> Here is how SERVICE works. Step 1: STEP. Step 2: STEP. Step 3: STEP. Ready to start? HOW TO BEGIN.

**Example:** "Here is how our repair service works. Step 1: bring in the item. Step 2: we give you a price within a day. Step 3: you approve it and we fix it. Ready to start? Drop in any weekday."

## Template 12: The announcement of a change

**Use it to:** share news such as new hours.

> A change from DATE: WHAT IS CHANGING. Why: REASON. What stays the same: WHAT STAYS. Questions? HOW TO ASK.

**Example:** "A change from May 1: we will open at 8 a.m. instead of 9 a.m. Why: many of you asked for an earlier coffee. What stays the same: our prices and our menu. Questions? Send us a message."

## Making a template sound like you

- **Use your own words for the blanks.** Say "loaf" if you say "loaf," not "baked product."
- **Keep one idea per post.** If a template tempts you to add three offers, split it into three posts.
- **Add one detail only you know.** A name, a smell, a small joke, or a number from your day.
- **Vary the order.** Do not post the same template twice in a row.
- **Check the length.** Most of these fit in two to five short sentences. Our guide on [how long a Facebook post should be](/blog/how-long-should-a-facebook-post-be) has more.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Leaving a blank unfilled.** Read every post once more before you publish. A stray "PRICE" in a live post looks careless.
- **Using the same template every day.** Mix at least three types in a week.
- **Making false claims.** Offers, dates, and facts must be true.
- **Skipping the next step.** Tell people what to do after reading.
- **Forgetting a photo.** A clear picture helps almost every template above.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will templates make my Page look generic?

Only if you leave them unchanged. Fill in real details, add a personal line, and mix the formats. The structure is a skeleton, and your details are the substance.

### How many templates should I rotate?

Start with five or six that fit your business. Use each about once or twice a month.

### Can I save my own templates?

Yes. When a post of yours does well, remove the specifics and keep the shape. You now have a template that already suits your audience.

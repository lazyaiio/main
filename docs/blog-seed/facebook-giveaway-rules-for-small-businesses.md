A giveaway is one of the simplest ways to get people talking on your Page. It is also one of the easiest ways to get into trouble. This guide explains the main Facebook giveaway rules in plain English, gives you a checklist for your own official rules, and shows a fair way to pick a winner.

One important note first. We are not lawyers, and this is not legal advice. Meta can change its policies, and the law about prize promotions differs from country to country. Read Meta's current policy yourself before every giveaway, and ask a local lawyer if the prize is valuable.

## Why run a giveaway at all

A good giveaway has a goal beyond "more likes." Pick one before you start.

- **Introduce a new product.** Offer it as the prize and let people see it.
- **Reward your community.** Thank the people who already follow you.
- **Collect ideas.** Ask a question and use the answers.
- **Reach new people.** Partner with a nearby business and share the prize.

Decide how you will measure success, too. It might be comments, new followers, or visits to your shop. Our [follower growth calculator](/tools/follower-growth-calculator) can help you see the change afterward.

## What Meta requires

Meta publishes rules for Pages in its Pages, Groups and Events Policies. Section 7 covers promotions, which Meta defines as a contest, giveaway, or sweepstakes. We read the official text, and these are the main points.

- **You are responsible for the law.** You must make sure the promotion follows the legal and regulatory requirements that apply. Meta's examples include giving entrants a copy of the official rules, stating the offer terms and who can enter, and registering the promotion with the authorities where that is required.
- **Entrants must release Meta.** You must require participants to fully release Meta from liability.
- **You must say Meta is not involved.** Participants must acknowledge that the promotion is in no way sponsored, endorsed, run by, or associated with Meta.
- **Meta will not help you run it.** Meta says it does not assist with administering a promotion. Picking winners and sending prizes is your job.

Recent guides from other companies also report two more limits, and we could not read them in the same official text. Meta's spam rules are said to forbid giving entries for likes, follows, or shares, and promotions are said to belong on a Page, group, or event, not on a personal profile. Treat both as likely and check them before you publish. The safest design is simple: do not require or reward likes, shares, or tags.

## Write your official rules

Your official rules are a short page or post that every entrant can read. Write them before the giveaway starts. Here is a checklist.

1. **Who can enter.** State the minimum age and the countries or cities where people may live.
2. **How to enter.** Describe one clear action, such as commenting on the giveaway post with a short answer.
3. **Start and end dates and times.** Include the time zone. Our [time zone converter](/tools/time-zone-converter) helps if your audience lives in several places.
4. **The prize.** Describe it exactly, including its value if the law requires you to state it, and say whether you will ship it.
5. **How the winner is chosen.** For a random draw, say so. For a contest where you judge entries, explain the criteria.
6. **How and when you will contact the winner.** Say what happens if the winner does not reply, for example that you will choose a backup winner after a set number of days.
7. **The Meta statements.** Include the release and the line that Meta does not sponsor, endorse, run, or have anything to do with the promotion.
8. **Who you are.** Give your business name and a way to contact you.
9. **Your own limits.** For example, one entry per person, and no employees or their families.

Keep the language simple. If a long rule is hard to read, many entrants will not read it, and that causes arguments later.

## Choose an entry method that is easy to run

The simplest method is a comment. You post the prize and a question, such as "What would you bake first with this mixer?" People answer in the comments. That gives you real entries and real ideas.

Avoid these designs.

- **Share to enter.** It pulls people's friends into your promotion, and Meta's rules are reported to forbid it.
- **Tag three friends.** It has the same problem and it annoys people.
- **Like the Page to enter.** It exchanges a prize for a like, which Meta's spam rules are reported to forbid.
- **Complicated forms with too much personal data.** Ask only for what you need to contact a winner.

Keep the entry period short. Three to seven days is usually enough to keep the post fresh.

## Pick the winner fairly

Fairness matters more than speed. People remember a giveaway that felt rigged.

1. **Close the entries at the time you promised.** Do not accept late ones.
2. **Make a list.** Copy the names of everyone who entered correctly into one list, one name per line.
3. **Count each person once** unless your rules say otherwise.
4. **Draw at random.** Use a tool that picks names with a secure random source. Our [giveaway winner picker](/tools/giveaway-winner-picker) does this in your browser and stores nothing.
5. **Choose backups.** Draw one or two backup winners and use them in order if the first winner does not reply in time.
6. **Keep a record.** Save a screenshot of the list and the draw with the date.

## Announce the winner

Announce the result in a new post, and say how the winner was chosen. You can use wording like this and change it to fit your Page.

"Our giveaway is closed, and the winner is Ana R. We drew the name at random from all the valid comments. Congratulations, Ana! We have sent you a message. If we do not hear back by Friday, we will choose a backup winner. Thank you to everyone who joined in. This promotion is in no way sponsored, endorsed, or run by Facebook or Meta."

Send the winner a direct message as well. Ask them for a delivery address only after they accept the prize. Never ask for passwords, payment card numbers, or anything similar. Scammers copy giveaway posts, so tell people that you will never ask for money to send a prize.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Changing the rules halfway.** If you must change something, explain why, and do it as early as possible.
- **Forgetting the Meta statements.** They are required, and they are easy to add.
- **Offering a prize you cannot afford to ship.** Check delivery costs first.
- **Ignoring local law.** Some places require permits or registration for prize promotions.
- **Using a personal profile.** Run it from your business Page.
- **Choosing the winner by hand.** Even if you are fair, a random draw is easier to defend.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are giveaways allowed on Facebook?** Yes. Meta allows promotions on Pages if you follow its policies and the law. Read the current rules before you start.

**Do I need official rules?** Meta's policy gives providing the official rules as an example of following the law, and most places expect them. Write them even for a small prize.

**Can I ask people to share the post?** You can invite people to share, but we advise against requiring or rewarding it. Check Meta's current rules.

**Can Facebook pick the winner for me?** No. Meta says it does not assist with administering promotions, so you choose and notify the winner yourself.

**Is a giveaway worth it?** It can be, when the prize fits your business and you measure the result. A giveaway that attracts people who only want free things may not lead to sales.

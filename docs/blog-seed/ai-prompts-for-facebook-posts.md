An AI writing tool is only as good as the instructions you give it. Type "write a Facebook post" and you will get something bland that could belong to any business. Give it real details and clear limits, and the draft gets much closer to something you can use. This guide gives you a simple formula and twenty AI prompts for Facebook posts that you can copy and adapt.

A warning first. AI drafts can be wrong, flat, or too eager. You are the one who answers for what gets published, so read and edit every draft. We cover the thinking behind that in our guide on [using AI to write Facebook posts without sounding robotic](/blog/how-to-use-ai-to-write-facebook-posts). This article is the practical toolkit that goes with it.

## The six-part prompt formula

A good prompt answers six questions. You do not need every one each time, but the more you give, the better the draft.

| Part | What to say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Who you are | Your business and what you sell | "I run a small bakery in a town of 20,000 people." |
| The audience | Who will read it | "Local families and office workers." |
| The goal | What the post should do | "Get people to order a birthday cake by Friday." |
| The facts | Real details the AI must use | "Cakes start at $35. Orders close Friday at 5 p.m." |
| The voice | Tone and style | "Warm, plain, a little playful. No hype." |
| The limits | Length and things to avoid | "Under 60 words. No emojis. No made-up claims." |

Put it together as a short paragraph, then add the task: "Write one Facebook post."

## A starter prompt you can reuse

Copy this, and fill in the capital letters:

> I run BUSINESS TYPE in PLACE. My readers are AUDIENCE. Write one Facebook post to GOAL. Use only these facts: FACTS. Voice: TONE. Keep it under NUMBER words. Start with a clear first line, end with one next step, and do not invent any details.

The last sentence matters. Telling the tool not to invent details cuts down on made-up claims, though it does not remove the risk, so you still need to check.

## Twenty prompts to copy

### Ideas and planning (prompts 1 to 5)

1. "Give me ten Facebook post ideas for a TYPE OF BUSINESS to use this month. Mix tips, behind-the-scenes, questions, and one offer. One line each."
2. "Suggest a four-week posting plan for a TYPE OF BUSINESS that posts three times a week. List the post type for each day."
3. "List twelve questions customers of a TYPE OF BUSINESS often ask. I will turn each into a post."
4. "Give me five post ideas tied to SEASON OR EVENT for a TYPE OF BUSINESS in PLACE."
5. "Turn this article summary into five separate Facebook posts, each with a different angle: SUMMARY."

### Writing a first draft (prompts 6 to 12)

6. "Write a Facebook post announcing NEW PRODUCT. Facts: FACTS. Keep it to three short sentences."
7. "Write a post that shares one helpful tip about TOPIC for AUDIENCE. Explain why it works in one sentence."
8. "Write a post that asks a friendly question about TOPIC to start a conversation. Keep it to two sentences."
9. "Write a short behind-the-scenes post about PERSON doing TASK. Warm tone, no hype."
10. "Write a post announcing new opening hours: NEW HOURS from DATE. Mention what stays the same."
11. "Write a thank-you post to customers after EVENT. Mention one specific thing that happened: DETAIL."
12. "Write a post about our offer: DETAILS. Include the exact end date and any limits. No pressure language."

### Improving what you wrote (prompts 13 to 17)

13. "Here is my draft: DRAFT. Rewrite it with a stronger first line, shorter sentences, and one clear next step. Keep my meaning."
14. "Give me five different first lines for this post: DRAFT. Make each one clear and specific. No clickbait."
15. "Shorten this post to under 50 words without losing the key facts: DRAFT."
16. "Rewrite this post in a warmer, more conversational voice: DRAFT."
17. "Point out anything unclear or missing in this post, and list questions a customer might still have: DRAFT."

### Reusing and replying (prompts 18 to 20)

18. "Rewrite this post as a version for a different day, with a new first line and the same facts: DRAFT."
19. "Draft a polite reply to this customer comment: COMMENT. Thank them, answer the question, and invite a message if needed. Facts you may use: FACTS."
20. "Draft a calm reply to this negative review: REVIEW. Apologize for the experience, do not argue, and ask them to send us a message. Do not include private details."

## Make the draft sound like you

AI writing has habits: stock phrases, too much excitement, and a tidy but empty tone. Fix those in the edit.

- **Add a detail only you know.** A name, a smell, a number from today.
- **Cut the filler.** Remove openings like "We are thrilled to announce."
- **Swap grand words for plain ones.** "Use" instead of "utilize," "help" instead of "empower."
- **Read it aloud.** If you would not say it, change it.
- **Check the emoji.** Keep them few and meaningful, or none.

You can also give the tool a sample of your own writing: "Here are three posts I wrote. Match this voice." Use posts you are happy with.

## A checklist before you publish

- Every fact is true: prices, dates, hours, names.
- Nothing was invented about your products, staff, or results.
- No private customer details appear.
- The first line makes sense on its own.
- There is one clear next step.
- It sounds like you.

## What not to put in a prompt

Treat a prompt as something that leaves your computer. Do not paste passwords, payment details, government ID numbers, or private information about customers or staff. When you must refer to a customer, remove their name and details. Read the terms and privacy notes of any tool you use, so you know what it does with your text.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Vague prompts.** "Write a post about my shop" gets a vague post.
- **No facts.** If you do not supply the details, the tool may invent them.
- **Publishing the first draft.** Always edit.
- **Using the same prompt forever.** Update your facts, tone, and goals as your business changes.
- **Letting AI make claims.** Never let a draft promise results, health benefits, or savings you cannot back up.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a special tool to use these prompts?

No. They work in most AI writing tools. A tool built for Facebook posts, such as [LazyAI](/pricing), can also keep your business details, tone, and past posts in one place, so you do not retype them each time.

### How long should a prompt be?

Long enough to include the facts and limits. A short paragraph is usually enough.

### Can AI write a month of posts for me?

It can give you a first draft of each. Plan the month first, then draft in a batch, and edit each post. Our guide on [scheduling a month of Facebook posts in one hour](/blog/how-to-schedule-a-month-of-facebook-posts-in-one-hour) shows a workable routine.

### How do I keep the posts from sounding the same?

Vary the post types, give the tool new facts each time, and add your own detail in the edit.

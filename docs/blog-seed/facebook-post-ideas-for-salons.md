A salon sells something that people judge by sight. Clients want to see the work, meet the team, and feel sure that they will be treated well. That makes Facebook a natural fit, but it also raises a question every owner asks: what do I post this week? This guide gives you thirty Facebook post ideas for salons, barbershops, and nail studios, plus a sample week and a few rules that keep your Page professional and respectful of your clients.

These are our own suggestions, based on common salon routines. We cannot promise results, and your clients may like different things. The way to learn is to post, read your numbers, and keep what earns messages and bookings. For the method behind a full month of posts, see our guide on [how to plan a month of Facebook content](/blog/how-to-plan-a-month-of-facebook-content).

## What a salon Page is for

A salon Page does four jobs. It shows the quality of your work. It introduces the people behind the chairs. It answers practical questions about prices, hours, and bookings. And it reminds past clients to come back.

Because clients often book by message, your best posts make the next step easy. Say how to book, say what a service costs or where to find prices, and keep the tone friendly. Pretty photos open the door, but clear details help people walk through it.

## Thirty post ideas, grouped by goal

### Show the work (ideas 1 to 8)

1. **A finished look.** One clear photo of a cut, color, or style, with the service name. Get the client's permission first.
2. **A tool or product spotlight.** Show one product you use and what it is good for.
3. **A before and after.** Only with written or clear spoken permission, and with the face hidden if the client prefers.
4. **A technique close-up.** A short video or photo of a precise cut, a braid, or a nail design.
5. **A seasonal style.** Show a look that suits the weather or an upcoming event.
6. **A tutorial in three steps.** For example, how to refresh a style at home between visits.
7. **A color story.** Show the colors you used and why they suit that client.
8. **Your studio.** A tidy photo of the space so new clients know what to expect.

### Show the people (ideas 9 to 15)

9. **Meet the stylist.** A photo, a favorite service, and how long they have worked with you.
10. **A team training day.** Show that your team keeps learning.
11. **A day in the salon.** Three photos from opening to the last appointment.
12. **A client thank-you.** Thank regulars without naming anyone who has not agreed to it.
13. **A work anniversary.** Celebrate a team member, with their agreement.
14. **A community tie-in.** Show a local event you joined or supported.
15. **A story about why you started.** Keep it short, real, and warm.

### Answer common questions (ideas 16 to 22)

16. **Opening hours and holiday changes.** Post them clearly and repeat when they change.
17. **How to book.** Message, phone, or online, and how far ahead to ask.
18. **Price guide.** A simple starting-price list, with a note that final prices depend on the service.
19. **Cancellation policy.** Explain it kindly and clearly, so there are no surprises.
20. **First-visit guide.** What to expect and how long it takes.
21. **Aftercare tips.** Practical advice for keeping a style or color looking good. Keep claims modest and accurate.
22. **Gift vouchers.** Where to buy them and how they work.

### Invite people in (ideas 23 to 30)

23. **A quiet-day offer.** A real, limited offer on a slow day, with exact dates.
24. **A new service.** Announce it with the price, the length, and how to book.
25. **A last-minute slot.** "Two openings tomorrow afternoon. Message us to book."
26. **A question post.** "Which would you like to see next: a trim tip or a color guide?"
27. **A referral thank-you.** If you have a referral scheme, explain it plainly.
28. **A wedding or event package.** Show what is included and how far ahead to book.
29. **A review thank-you.** Share a kind comment, with the client's permission, and thank them.
30. **A reminder.** "Haven't been in for a while? Message us for your next slot."

## A sample week you can copy

Here is one example week for a salon that posts four times. Move the days to match when your clients decide to book.

| Day | Post type | Idea from the list |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Hours and a booking reminder | 16 and 17 |
| Wednesday | A finished look | 1 or 4 |
| Friday | A people or behind-the-scenes post | 9 or 11 |
| Sunday | A question or a last-minute slot | 26 or 25 |

If four posts feels like too many, start with two. Our guide on [how often to post on Facebook](/blog/how-often-should-you-post-on-facebook) can help you choose a rhythm you can keep.

## Client photos and privacy

Photos of work are your strongest posts, and they belong partly to your client. A few habits protect both of you.

- **Ask first, every time.** Get a clear yes before you post a photo, and keep a note of it.
- **Offer a face-free option.** Many clients are happy to show their hair or nails but not their face.
- **Do not tag or name clients** unless they ask you to.
- **Take the photo down if asked.** Do it quickly and politely.
- **Never share private details,** such as health information or what a client said in confidence.
- **Follow your local rules** about photos and consent, as they differ from place to place.

## Writing tips for salon posts

- **Lead with the service,** not a slogan. "Balayage in one visit" tells people more than "Obsessed with this look."
- **Keep claims honest.** Do not promise results you cannot guarantee.
- **Add one next step.** Book, message, or visit.
- **Use light and a clean background** for photos.
- **Reply to comments and messages quickly.** Our guide on [replying to Facebook comments and reviews](/blog/how-to-reply-to-facebook-comments-and-reviews) has examples.

For help with openings and endings, read our guide to [hooks and calls to action](/blog/facebook-post-hooks-and-calls-to-action).

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Posting only discounts.** Clients start to wait for the next offer.
- **Using photos that are not yours.** Stock photos or another salon's work can mislead.
- **Posting a client without permission.** It can hurt trust, and in some places it breaks the law.
- **Leaving prices out for months.** People will not ask if they feel awkward.
- **Forgetting to update hours.** Wrong hours lose bookings.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a salon post on Facebook?

Choose a number you can keep. For many small salons that is two to four posts a week. A steady schedule beats a flurry followed by silence.

### Should I post my prices?

A clear starting-price guide helps people decide to book. Say that final prices depend on the service and the length of the appointment.

### Can I use the same photos on Facebook and other sites?

Yes, if the client agreed to it. Say where you may use the photo when you ask for permission.

### Can I plan a month of salon posts at once?

Yes. Choose the ideas above that fit your services, give each a day, and write them in one sitting. Our free [content calendar generator](/tools/content-calendar-generator) can lay out the month for you.

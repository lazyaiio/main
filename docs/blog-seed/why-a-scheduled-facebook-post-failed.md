You planned the post, wrote it, and scheduled it for Tuesday morning. On Tuesday afternoon you look at your Page and find nothing. A scheduled Facebook post failed, and you do not know why. It is frustrating, but it is also common, and the cause is usually one of a handful of things.

This guide walks through the most common reasons, shows how to check each one, and gives you a routine that makes failures rare and easy to catch. The causes below come from how scheduling works in general. The exact message you see depends on the tool you use and on what Facebook returns, so we cannot cover every case. If your problem is not here, the first place to look is the status or error shown next to the post. For the full picture of how scheduling works, see our guide on [how to schedule Facebook Page posts](/blog/how-to-schedule-facebook-page-posts).

## First, find out what really happened

Before you change anything, find the facts.

1. **Look at the post's status.** Most tools show whether a post is scheduled, published, or failed.
2. **Read the message.** If an error is shown, copy it down exactly.
3. **Check your Page directly.** Open the Page in a browser or in Meta Business Suite and see whether the post is there, was held back, or is missing.
4. **Check the time.** Compare the scheduled time with the current time in your time zone.
5. **Note what changed.** Did you change a password, a Page role, or a setting just before the failure?

Those five checks solve many cases on their own.

## The most common causes

| Cause | What it looks like | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| The connection expired or lost permission | An error about access or authorization | Your scheduling tool's connection page |
| Your role on the Page changed | You can no longer post for the Page | Page roles in Facebook or Meta Business Suite |
| A problem with the picture or video | Media failed to upload or was rejected | The file's type, size, and shape |
| A problem with a link | The link is broken, blocked, or slow | Open the link on a phone |
| Content was flagged | The post was removed or held for review | Your Page's quality and support area |
| The time was wrong | The post went out at an odd hour, or not at all | Time zone settings and the schedule |
| A temporary problem on either side | A brief error that goes away | Try again later |

### 1. The connection expired or lost permission

A scheduling tool acts for you through a connection to your Page. That connection can stop working. It may happen after you change your Facebook password, when you remove the tool from your Facebook settings, or when your role on the Page changes. It can also happen when permissions expire or need to be granted again.

**How to fix it:** reconnect the Page in your scheduling tool and approve the permissions it asks for. In LazyAI, a Page that cannot be reached shows a notice that it needs to be reconnected, and a Reconnect button takes you through it again. There is also a notification setting called "Facebook Page needs attention," so you can hear about it early.

### 2. Your role on the Page changed

To publish for a Page, you need a role that allows it. If someone changed your role, or you were removed, posting will fail. This is easy to miss when a team changes.

**How to fix it:** ask a Page admin to check your role, and ask them to restore the access you need.

### 3. A problem with the picture or video

Media is a frequent cause. A file may be too large, in an unsupported format, or damaged. A video may take too long to process. A picture may fail to upload on a slow connection.

**How to fix it:**

- Save the picture again as a standard JPG or PNG.
- Reduce very large files before you upload them.
- Try a different file to see if the original is the problem.
- For video, upload a shorter or lighter file as a test.

Limits differ by tool and change over time, so check your tool's current help pages rather than trusting a number from an old article.

### 4. A problem with a link

If a post has a link, the link has to work. A mistyped address, a page that is down, or a site that blocks previews can cause trouble. Sometimes the post publishes but the preview is empty or wrong.

**How to fix it:** open the link on a phone, on mobile data, and make sure it loads. If the preview looks wrong, check the page's title and image settings on your website.

### 5. The post was flagged or held

Facebook may hold or remove content that appears to break its rules. This can happen to a post with text or images that look like spam or misleading offers, or that break another community rule. A post can be flagged by mistake.

**How to fix it:** read the notice on your Page, and read the relevant policy. If you think it was a mistake, use the appeal option if one is shown. Then rewrite the post so it is clear and honest. Our guide on [Facebook giveaway rules for small businesses](/blog/facebook-giveaway-rules-for-small-businesses) covers one area where small Pages often run into rules.

### 6. The time was wrong

A time zone mix-up can make a post go out hours early or late. If the time you chose is already past, a tool may publish it at once, ask you to change it, or reject it.

**How to fix it:** check the time zone on your account and in your tool, and make sure both match where you are. The free [time zone converter](/tools/time-zone-converter) helps if you plan for an audience in another place. For choosing good times, see our guide on the [best time to post on Facebook](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-facebook).

### 7. A temporary problem

Sometimes a service has a short outage, or a request times out. The post fails once and works the next time.

**How to fix it:** wait a few minutes and retry. If it keeps failing, note the error and contact your tool's support.

## A step-by-step fix routine

1. Read the status and the error message.
2. Check the Page itself for the post.
3. Confirm the connection and your role.
4. Check the media and the link.
5. Check the time and the time zone.
6. Edit what you found, and schedule the post again or publish it by hand.
7. Write down the cause and the fix in a short log.

If the post was time-sensitive, such as a sale ending today, publish it by hand first and sort out the cause afterward.

## How to prevent failures

- **Schedule a few days ahead,** not minutes ahead, so you have time to fix a problem.
- **Check your week every Monday.** Look at what is scheduled and what published.
- **Keep two people with access** to the Page, so a role change does not stop you.
- **Review connections once a month.** Reconnect before something breaks, especially after a password change.
- **Test new media types** with one post before you schedule a full week.
- **Keep a short log** of failures and fixes. Patterns show up quickly.

A weekly check pairs well with a planning habit. Our guide on [scheduling a month of Facebook posts in one hour](/blog/how-to-schedule-a-month-of-facebook-posts-in-one-hour) shows how to build one.

## When to contact support

Contact your scheduling tool's support when:

- the same error returns after you reconnect and retry;
- several posts fail in a row for no clear reason;
- the tool says a post was published, but you cannot find it on your Page.

Send them the post's time, the Page name, and the exact error message. That saves a lot of back and forth.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will a failed post be retried automatically?

That depends on the tool and the cause. Some tools retry a short problem, and some stop at the first error. Check your tool's help pages, and do not assume a retry will happen.

### Can I find out why Facebook rejected a post?

Sometimes. Check the notice on your Page and the status in your tool. If a reason is not shown, the steps above will cover most causes.

### Do failed posts count against me?

A technical failure by itself does not harm your Page. A post that breaks content rules is a different matter, so read the rules and fix the post.
